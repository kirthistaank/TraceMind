================================================================================
                     TRACEMIND PRESET SCENARIOS
                   Clinical Triage Test Cases (12 Scenarios)
================================================================================

This document contains all 12 preset clinical scenarios organized by condition.
Each scenario includes multi-turn conversation examples that demonstrate different
severity levels (Home Management, Urgent Same-Day, ER Now) across 4 conditions.

USAGE OPTIONS:
1. INDIVIDUAL TURNS: Submit each turn separately for multi-turn reasoning
2. COMBINED SCENARIO: Submit all turns as a single message for quick testing

KEY ABBREVIATIONS:
- CPG: Clinical Practice Guideline
- SpO2: Oxygen Saturation
- RR: Respiratory Rate
- ER: Emergency Room / Emergency Department

================================================================================
FEVER (3 Scenarios)
================================================================================

---
SCENARIO 1: 3-year-old with mild fever (Home Care)
Disposition: HOME_MANAGEMENT
Description: Mild fever, normal vitals, alert and playful — safe for home management.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 3-year-old has a fever and mild cough

Turn 2:
Temp is 100.8, eating normally, drinking okay, breathing normally

Turn 3:
He's playful and answering me, peed a couple hours ago

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 3-year-old has a fever and mild cough Temp is 100.8, eating normally, drinking okay, breathing normally He's playful and answering me, peed a couple hours ago

Expected Rules Fired:
Conservative home management

---

---
SCENARIO 2: 5-year-old with repeated vomiting (Urgent)
Disposition: URGENT_SAME_DAY
Description: Persistent vomiting, low fluid intake — needs same-day care.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
5 year old fever and vomiting

Turn 2:
He vomited 4 times in the last 2 hours and only sips

Turn 3:
102.5 fever, breathing fine, answers questions, he keeps throwing up and won't drink much, he peed an hour ago

COMBINED SCENARIO:
5 year old fever and vomiting He vomited 4 times in the last 2 hours and only sips 102.5 fever, breathing fine, answers questions, he keeps throwing up and won't drink much, he peed an hour ago

Expected Rules Fired:
Repeated vomiting with poor fluid intake

---

---
SCENARIO 3: 6-year-old with lethargy (ER Now)
Disposition: ER_NOW
Description: Lethargy, dehydration risk — go to ER immediately.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 6-year-old has a fever, threw up, and looks really wiped out. I'm worried.

Turn 2:
Temp is 103.5. He's barely responding, just lying there. He doesn't want to drink. No trouble breathing. Also, there's been a stomach virus going around his school this week.

Turn 3:
I don't think he's peed since this afternoon.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 6-year-old has a fever, threw up, and looks really wiped out. I'm worried. Temp is 103.5. He's barely responding, just lying there. He doesn't want to drink. No trouble breathing. Also, there's been a stomach virus going around his school this week. I don't think he's peed since this afternoon.

Expected Rules Fired:
Severe dehydration, Altered alertness

---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 4-year-old has some wheezing

He's speaking in full sentences, playing, no major distress

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 4-year-old has some wheezing He's speaking in full sentences, playing, no major distress O2 sat is 97%, normal energy, no retractions

Mild asthma exacerbation, Mild wheeze without distress

---

---

Disposition: URGENT_SAME_DAY

My 7-year-old is having trouble breathing, some wheezing

Turn 2:
He's only able to say short phrases, has some chest retractions

Turn 3:
O2 sat is 92%, you can see his ribs pulling in when he breathes

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 7-year-old is having trouble breathing, some wheezing He's only able to say short phrases, has some chest retractions O2 sat is 92%, you can see his ribs pulling in when he breathes

Expected Rules Fired:
Moderate asthma exacerbation, Moderate retractions with asthma

ANAPHYLAXIS / ALLERGIC REACTION (3 Scenarios)
================================================================================

---
SCENARIO 7: 3-year-old with mild hives (Home Care)
Disposition: HOME_MANAGEMENT
Description: Localized hives only, normal breathing, alert — monitor at home.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 3-year-old just ate peanuts for the first time and now has some itchy spots on his face and neck.

Turn 2:
He's alert and happy. Breathing is normal. No swelling. Just little red bumps where he scratched. Temperature is normal.

Turn 3:
No difficulty swallowing. He's still playful. The hives are getting less itchy already. No known peanut allergy history.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 3-year-old just ate peanuts for the first time and now has some itchy spots on his face and neck. He's alert and happy. Breathing is normal. No swelling. Just little red bumps where he scratched. Temperature is normal. No difficulty swallowing. He's still playful. The hives are getting less itchy already. No known peanut allergy history.

Expected Rules Fired:
Mild localized allergic reaction

---

---
SCENARIO 8: 6-year-old with angioedema (Urgent)
Disposition: URGENT_SAME_DAY
Description: Face/lip swelling, hives, thick tongue feeling — urgent evaluation needed.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 6-year-old had shellfish at dinner and now her lips are swelling up and she's getting hives all over.

Turn 2:
She says her tongue feels thick. She's alert and breathing OK but I can see swelling around her mouth and on her face. No stridor yet. She's breathing normally.

Turn 3:
She's never had a shellfish allergy before. Temperature is normal. No wheezing. She's getting more hives on her chest.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 6-year-old had shellfish at dinner and now her lips are swelling up and she's getting hives all over. She says her tongue feels thick. She's alert and breathing OK but I can see swelling around her mouth and on her face. No stridor yet. She's breathing normally. She's never had a shellfish allergy before. Temperature is normal. No wheezing. She's getting more hives on her chest.

Expected Rules Fired:
Significant allergic reaction

---

---
SCENARIO 9: 4-year-old with anaphylaxis (ER Now)
Disposition: ER_NOW
Description: Wheezing, stridor, facial swelling, 92% SpO2, dizziness — CALL 911.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 4-year-old just ate a bug and now he's having trouble breathing. He's wheezing and his face is really swollen.

Turn 2:
He has hives all over his body. He says he feels dizzy. Oxygen is 92%. He's wheezing and has some stridor. His lips and tongue are very swollen. He's alert but seems confused.

Turn 3:
He's breathing hard and doesn't look right. No known bee allergy. This is happening really fast.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 4-year-old just ate a bug and now he's having trouble breathing. He's wheezing and his face is really swollen. He has hives all over his body. He says he feels dizzy. Oxygen is 92%. He's wheezing and has some stridor. His lips and tongue are very swollen. He's alert but seems confused. He's breathing hard and doesn't look right. No known bee allergy. This is happening really fast.

Expected Rules Fired:
Anaphylaxis - multi-system involvement, Anaphylaxis with airway involvement

CROUP (3 Scenarios)
================================================================================

---
SCENARIO 10: 2-year-old with mild croup (Home Care)
Disposition: HOME_MANAGEMENT
Description: Barky cough, mild stridor with crying only, normal oxygen — manage at home.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 2-year-old has a barky cough that sounds like a seal. He's been sick for 2 days.

Turn 2:
No fever or very low-grade. He has a barky cough and some mild stridor when he cries. He's playing between cough fits. Breathing is normal at rest with no retractions.

Turn 3:
Oxygen is 96%. He's happy and alert. Eating and drinking normally. The cough is worse at night but he seems fine during the day.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 2-year-old has a barky cough that sounds like a seal. He's been sick for 2 days. No fever or very low-grade. He has a barky cough and some mild stridor when he cries. He's playing between cough fits. Breathing is normal at rest with no retractions. Oxygen is 96%. He's happy and alert. Eating and drinking normally. The cough is worse at night but he seems fine during the day.

Expected Rules Fired:
Mild croup, Barky cough without stridor

---

---
SCENARIO 11: 18-month-old with moderate croup (Urgent)
Disposition: URGENT_SAME_DAY
Description: Stridor at rest, chest retractions, 94% SpO2 — urgent care needed.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 18-month-old has had a barky cough for 2 days and now has a different sounding breathing.

Turn 2:
Low-grade fever. He has stridor even at rest now. I can see his chest pulling in when he breathes. He seems tired and fussy. Oxygen is 94%.

Turn 3:
He's still alert but quieter than usual. He's not eating much. The stridor is getting worse. He has croup and his sibling has it too.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 18-month-old has had a barky cough for 2 days and now has a different sounding breathing. Low-grade fever. He has stridor even at rest now. I can see his chest pulling in when he breathes. He seems tired and fussy. Oxygen is 94%. He's still alert but quieter than usual. He's not eating much. The stridor is getting worse. He has croup and his sibling has it too.

Expected Rules Fired:
Moderate croup, Croup with moderate retractions

---

---
SCENARIO 12: 3-year-old with severe croup (ER Now)
Disposition: ER_NOW
Description: Severe stridor at rest, severe retractions, 89% SpO2, pale lips — CALL 911.
---

INDIVIDUAL TURNS:

Turn 1:
My 3-year-old woke up with terrible barky cough and can barely breathe. He's very distressed and I'm really scared.

Turn 2:
No high fever. But he has loud stridor even at rest. His chest and neck are pulling in with every breath. Oxygen is 89%. He's sitting still, not playing, just focused on breathing.

Turn 3:
He looks very ill. He's only able to say one word at a time. His lips look pale. He has had croup before but never this bad. I'm calling 911.

COMBINED SCENARIO:
My 3-year-old woke up with terrible barky cough and can barely breathe. He's very distressed and I'm really scared. No high fever. But he has loud stridor even at rest. His chest and neck are pulling in with every breath. Oxygen is 89%. He's sitting still, not playing, just focused on breathing. He looks very ill. He's only able to say one word at a time. His lips look pale. He has had croup before but never this bad. I'm calling 911.

Expected Rules Fired:
Severe croup, Croup with hypoxia, Croup with respiratory distress

USAGE INSTRUCTIONS
================================================================================

OPTION 1: INDIVIDUAL TURNS (Recommended for testing multi-turn reasoning)
- Submit each "Turn" message separately to the system
- Allows the system to accumulate information progressively across interactions
- Better demonstrates reasoning across multiple interactions
- More realistic simulation of actual parent-clinician conversation

OPTION 2: COMBINED SCENARIO (Quick testing & batch evaluation)
- Copy the "COMBINED SCENARIO" text and submit as a single message
- Tests extraction accuracy with complex, multi-symptom inputs
- Faster for batch testing and automation
- Useful for verifying extraction consistency

BOTH APPROACHES SHOULD YIELD THE SAME DISPOSITION:
✅ HOME_MANAGEMENT
✅ URGENT_SAME_DAY
✅ ER_NOW

If dispositions differ between approaches, it indicates a multi-turn accumulation or
extraction consistency issue that should be investigated.
